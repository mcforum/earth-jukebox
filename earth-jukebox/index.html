    What you hear

    How Earth Jukebox reads a picture

    NASA’s Earth pictures store their data as colour. Earth Jukebox knows the colour scale of each record, so it can turn the colour at any spot back into what it stands for, such as a water temperature or a kind of particle, and then say it and play it.

    Pointing
    The tone under your pointer gets higher as the value gets higher. Where there is no data, such as land on an ocean map, the tone stops. On flat world maps you also get the name of the sea or region and its coordinates.
    Playing
    The picture is split into a few parts, for example north, tropics and south. They play one after another, always in the same order, followed by a short pause. The part that sounds is outlined and labelled.
    A little, some, a lot
    For records that show an amount, the words compare each part with the busiest moment of that record so far. They are not exact measurements.
    Trying the sounds
    Under “What you hear” there is a button for each sound, so you can learn what cold and hot, or smoke and dust, sound like before you start.

    Nothing is recorded in advance. Every sound is made in the moment from the picture on screen.

    Keyboard

    Space play or pause. 1 to 8 pick a record. D describe the record. On the picture: arrow keys move the pointer, Shift moves it faster, Enter reads the spot out, Esc lets go.
